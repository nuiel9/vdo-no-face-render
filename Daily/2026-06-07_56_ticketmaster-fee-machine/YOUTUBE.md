## Description

In April 2026 a federal jury found Ticketmaster and Live Nation liable on every antitrust count — an illegal monopoly that overcharged fans for years. And your fees still aren't going down. This is the business postmortem of how the most hated checkout screen in America was actually built, and why winning in court can't take it apart.

The story: the Jan 25 2010 merger and its 10-year consent decree; the promotion → venues → ticketing flywheel that left fans no rival checkout; the GAO's 2018 finding that primary-market fees averaged 27% of the ticket price and ran as high as 58%; the five consent-decree violations and the 2020 "slap on the wrist"; the November 2022 Eras Tour presale meltdown (3.5 million Verified Fan registrations, a site that crashed within the hour, 2.4 million tickets sold, and a canceled general sale); the January 2023 Senate hearing; the May 2024 DOJ-and-states lawsuit; the March 2026 federal settlement (~$200M, no breakup) that several states refused; and the April 2026 jury verdict that changed the law but not the fee. The structural truth: when one company promotes the show, owns the venue, and sells the ticket, the fee isn't an overcharge bolted on — it's the business model. Which is exactly why a guilty verdict doesn't lower it.
