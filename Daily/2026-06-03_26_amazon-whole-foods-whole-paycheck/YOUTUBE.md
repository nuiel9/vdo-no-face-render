## Description

In 2017, Amazon paid $13.7 billion for Whole Foods and promised to do the one thing everyone wanted: kill the "Whole Paycheck" reputation. On day one it cut prices by as much as 43 percent. Seven years later, the nickname is still there and the prices are higher than ever. This video traces what actually happened: the August 28 2017 day-one price cuts, the May 2018 Prime-member discounts, the Gordon Haskett basket studies that found the cuts were narrower than the headlines, the early-2019 reversal when roughly 700 supplier contracts expired, and the structural reason the most powerful price-cutter in retail history could not make a premium grocer cheap. The lesson: sometimes a company's biggest weakness is just its actual product.
