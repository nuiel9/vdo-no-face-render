# YOUTUBE: The Churn Rate That Broke a $50 Billion Company
Row #38 | Peloton subscription collapse | UTM: business-pm-peloton-churn

Peloton went public at $29 a share in September 2019. By January 2021, the stock crossed $167 and the company was valued at $50 billion. Then, in fiscal year 2022, it reported a net loss of approximately $2.8 billion. The mainstream explanation is that COVID demand reversed. That is true. But the Peloton 10-K filings tell a different story: the subscription model required perpetual hardware sales to function. When hardware demand normalized, the subscriber acquisition pipeline dried up, and the monthly churn rate compounded against a base that had no way to grow. This is the structural case the SEC filings were making throughout the same period investors were pricing the company like a SaaS business.

## Pinned Comment Draft

Peloton's 10-K filings disclosed average net monthly connected fitness churn of approximately 0.73% in FY2021. That is roughly 9% annual churn. The subscriber base was growing because the pandemic was forcing hardware conversions at scale. When hardware sales fell by nearly $1 billion in a single fiscal year, there was no other mechanism to replace churning subscribers. The filings were documenting this in real time.
