## Description

In March 2007, Circuit City terminated 3,400 of its highest-paid hourly workers to cut costs. Twelve months later, it filed for Chapter 11. The firing is the headline most people remember. It was not the cause.

This case study covers the three decisions that actually killed Circuit City: the $114 million DIVX disc-format write-off in 1999, the exit from the appliances business around 2000, and the board-approved elimination of the service expertise that was the company's only pricing justification. Each decision looked defensible on a spreadsheet. Together they removed every advantage a 60-year-old retailer had built.

Based on SEC filings, U.S. bankruptcy court records, and W. Alan Wurtzel's 2012 postmortem.
