# REVIEW: Circuit City Outlawed Itself from Winning
Row #24 | Rise and Fall | 2026-05-10 | UTM: business-pm-24

---

## Thesis

Circuit City did not die because Amazon appeared or because Best Buy outspent it. It died because its own management team made three discrete, board-approved decisions that stripped away the only advantages the company had: a defensible service margin, a stable appliance revenue base, and an experienced frontline workforce. The 2007 mass layoff of 3,400 hourly workers was the moment the press noticed. The actual death warrant was signed years earlier in a Richmond boardroom, in decisions that have almost no coverage relative to the firing headline.

## Contrarian Angles (5 candidates)

1. **The DIVX gamble killed the balance sheet before Best Buy arrived.** Circuit City co-developed a proprietary pay-per-view DVD format (DIVX, not the open codec) in 1997-1998, forced it into stores, and abandoned it in 1999 at an approximately $114 million write-off. That was capital they could not reinvest in store modernization.

2. **Exiting appliances in 2000 was the structural wound.** Appliances generated predictable, margin-stable revenue and drove service contracts. Without them, Circuit City's revenue mix shifted entirely to high-velocity, low-margin electronics where Best Buy held scale advantages. This is the angle with the clearest cause-and-effect: no appliances, no margin buffer.

3. **CarMax proves Circuit City knew how to build good businesses.** The auto dealership chain Circuit City founded became one of the most durable retailers in America after its 2002 spin-off. The same management team that killed Circuit City also produced CarMax. That gap in outcomes deserves more scrutiny than it gets.

4. **The 3,400 firing destroyed customer service at the exact moment HDTV required it.** The workers fired in March 2007 were the company's most tenured, highest-rated hourly staff. Circuit City's differentiation from Walmart was "expert help." Removing expertise to save dollars on the income statement while HDTV was creating a once-in-a-generation upsell window was a category-level error.

5. **Schoonover's strategy was board-approved, not rogue.** Most Circuit City postmortems treat Philip Schoonover as a lone villain. The board approved each of his cost-cutting moves. This is a governance failure, not a rogue-CEO story, and it changes what lessons investors should draw.

**SELECTED: Angle 4.** The 3,400-firing moment is the consumer-grievance hook the algorithm will reward, but the real analytical pivot is that the firing destroyed Circuit City's only competitive moat (trained sales staff) at the precise moment when HDTV penetration was creating a window where expertise had the highest value. That irony is the script's spine.

## 6 Lesser-Known Facts

1. Samuel Wurtzel, Circuit City's founder, had no electronics background. He came from the import-export trade before opening "Wards Company" in Richmond, Virginia in 1949. (Source: Wurtzel, "Good to Great to Gone," Chapter 1)
2. Circuit City invented the consumer electronics superstore format, operating it decades before Best Buy adopted a version of it. (Source: trade press + Wurtzel book; contrast to Best Buy's 1966 founding as a single audio store)
3. The company's proprietary DIVX disc format (not to be confused with the open video codec) was abandoned in June 1999 after approximately $114 million in losses. (Source: Circuit City FY1999 10-K, write-off disclosure)
4. The 3,400 workers fired in March 2007 were explicitly told they could reapply for their jobs at a lower wage after a 10-week waiting period. The company confirmed this publicly. (Source: contemporary AP and Reuters wire coverage, March 28, 2007)
5. No acquirer emerged willing to buy Circuit City as a going concern during the bankruptcy process. Best Buy, Systemax, and others evaluated the assets and declined. (Source: Case 08-35653 docket, first-quarter 2009 filings)
6. CarMax, the auto dealership chain Circuit City founded, has a market capitalization today that is a multiple of Circuit City's peak valuation. The same parent that failed in electronics created one of the most durable retail businesses in America. (Source: CarMax NYSE public filings; Circuit City peak market cap per FY2000-era 10-K)
