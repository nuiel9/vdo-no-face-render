# REVIEW: MoviePass's $9.95 fraud against its own investors
Row #17 | Hidden Truth | 2026-05-11 | UTM: business-pm-17

---

## Thesis

The standard MoviePass story is that the unit economics were obviously broken: subscribers paid $9.95 a month, MoviePass paid theaters roughly $12 to $14 per ticket, and the company burned cash on every redemption. That is true and not the actual story. The actual story is that Helios and Matheson Analytics, the public company that acquired a majority stake in MoviePass in 2017, repeatedly told investors and analysts a version of the business that did not match the version it was running internally. Mitch Lowe, the MoviePass CEO, and Ted Farnsworth, the Helios and Matheson Analytics CEO, were charged by federal prosecutors in late 2022 with making materially misleading statements to the market. The customer-facing story (cheap movies) was a stunt. The investor-facing story was a securities case.

## Contrarian Angles (5 candidates)

1. **The fraud was not the price. The fraud was the disclosure.** Every business-press article focused on the $9.95 burn. The federal cases focus on what HMNY told markets between mid-2017 and mid-2018 about subscriber economics. Most viewers conflate the two.

2. **Mitch Lowe was a Netflix and Redbox veteran.** He was not a first-time operator who stumbled into a bad model. He had built two consumer subscription businesses before. The narrative that "they did not understand the unit economics" does not survive his resume.

3. **The $9.95 offer was a customer-acquisition stunt.** Internal communications cited in the SEC complaint suggest the unlimited tier was treated as a marketing line item, not a sustainable plan. The plan was to grow subscriber count, then monetize the audience separately through data, ads, and studio partnerships.

4. **Stacy Spikes, the original MoviePass founder, was pushed out before the $9.95 launch.** The company Spikes built and the company that imploded under Lowe and Farnsworth were operationally and culturally different. Spikes later bought the brand back out of bankruptcy and relaunched.

5. **The retail-investor cohort took most of the loss.** Helios and Matheson Analytics did multiple equity raises and reverse splits during the run. The dilution and the misrepresentation hit the same population: retail holders who did not have analyst desks decoding the filings.

**SELECTED: Angle 1.** The "fraud against the price" framing matches the YouTube algorithm consumer-grievance pattern (highest-performing template on Disclosed: McDonald's #30 at 659 long-form views) AND opens with a verifiable federal-case anchor. The disclosure-vs-price reframe is the contrarian hook the audience will not have heard.

## 6 Lesser-Known Facts

1. MoviePass was founded in 2011 by Stacy Spikes and Hamet Watt, years before Helios and Matheson Analytics was involved. The aggressive $9.95 unlimited tier launched in August 2017, after HMNY took majority control. (Source: SEC and trade press contemporaneous to August 2017)
2. Mitch Lowe was a Netflix cofounder and a Redbox president before joining MoviePass. Two prior consumer-subscription operator roles. (Source: corporate biography, multiple business press interviews 2017-2018)
3. Helios and Matheson Analytics traded under the ticker HMNY. The stock did multiple reverse splits in 2018 to maintain Nasdaq listing minimums. (Source: Nasdaq listing history, HMNY 8-K filings)
4. MoviePass introduced surge pricing, blackout periods, and rotating restrictions on titles during 2018 to slow burn. These changes were announced repeatedly, then partially walked back. (Source: trade press timeline 2018; HMNY 10-Q filings)
5. Helios and Matheson Analytics filed for Chapter 7 bankruptcy in January 2020. MoviePass ceased operations in September 2019. (Source: U.S. Bankruptcy Court records; press release archive)
6. Stacy Spikes, the original founder, repurchased the MoviePass brand out of the bankruptcy estate and relaunched it in 2022. The relaunched product is operated by a different entity from the HMNY-era company. (Source: 2022 trade press coverage)
