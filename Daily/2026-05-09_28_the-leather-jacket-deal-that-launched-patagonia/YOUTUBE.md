# YOUTUBE: The Company That Asked You Not to Buy Its Jacket
Row #28 | Patagonia origin story | UTM: business-pm-28

In 1957, Yvon Chouinard started making climbing pitons in a Burbank backyard and sold them at Yosemite for $1.50 each. By the late 1960s, Chouinard Equipment was the largest climbing hardware supplier in the United States. Then he published a 14-page catalog essay telling his customers to stop buying his most profitable product. This video traces the pattern that began with that essay: the 1986 pledge to donate 1% of revenue, the 2011 "Don't Buy This Jacket" Black Friday ad in the New York Times, and the 2022 transfer that made climate nonprofits Patagonia's only shareholders. Each decision cost something in the short term. Together they built a $3 billion company.
