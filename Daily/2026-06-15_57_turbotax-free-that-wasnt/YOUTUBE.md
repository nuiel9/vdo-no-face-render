## Description

Every spring the ads promised "free, free, free" — and somewhere in the middle, free quietly turned into $90. This is the business postmortem of how TurboTax's maker, Intuit, turned the word "free" into the most expensive word of tax season, and then spent a fortune making sure a truly free alternative never survived.

The story: the 2002 IRS Free File deal, in which the government agreed not to build its own free tool as long as the industry offered one; ProPublica's 2019 finding that Intuit added code hiding its genuinely-free filing page from search engines; the May 2022 settlement — $141 million to all 50 states for steering roughly 4.4 million customers into paying when they qualified to file free; the FTC's January 2024 order barring Intuit from advertising "free" when the agency found about two-thirds of taxpayers couldn't actually qualify; Intuit quitting the Free File program in 2021; the record lobbying (nearly $4 million in 2024); the IRS's 2024 Direct File tool that let people file simple returns free, directly with the government; and the number that tells the whole story — about 296,000 people used Direct File last year, and zero this year, after it was shut down for the 2026 season. The structural truth: the product was never tax software. It was the belief that filing has to be confusing and paid — and a fine is just the rent you pay to keep that belief alive.
